Questions

When is the deadline?

In Santa Cruz County, regular appeals for the 2026–27 roll can be filed from July 2 through November 30, 2026. Some California counties close September 15. Deadlines change, so we check them every year. Supplemental assessments have their own window: 60 days from the notice date.

Who decides my appeal?

The county Assessment Appeals Board. It can lower your assessment, leave it unchanged, or raise it. We'll tell you about that risk before you file. The board has up to two years to decide, and its decision is final. Further review goes through superior court.

Do I still have to pay my property taxes?

Yes. Pay on time. If you win, you get a refund of the overpaid tax. We tick the refund claim on your application so that happens automatically.

What is the evidence based on?

Your home's market value as of January 1 (the lien date), backed by comparable sales near that date. Close, similar homes make the strongest evidence. Condition issues like an old roof, damage, or aging systems can support a lower value too.

How can you file for me?

County rules let an agent file with your written authorization. You sign that during intake. You still e-sign the application's certification yourself, because it's made under penalty of perjury.

What does the county charge?

In Santa Cruz County, the e-file portal charges a $50 nonrefundable fee per application (2026). You file one application per parcel per year.

What's the Homeowner's Exemption?

A separate, one-time claim for your primary residence. It takes $7,000 off your assessed value, about $86 a year. Many owners never claim it. We check for it with every plan, or you can call the Assessor at (831) 454-2002.

Is this legal advice?

No. We're not a law firm. We prepare appeal materials and file as your agent. For legal questions, talk to an attorney.

Can I do it myself?

Yes. Any owner can file directly with the Clerk of the Board, (831) 454-2323. Our DIY kit handles the research and paperwork for you.
