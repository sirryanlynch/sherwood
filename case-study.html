The home

3 bed / 2 bath, 1,470 sq ft, built 1982. Bought in 2024; under Prop 13 its assessed value has since risen 2% a year.

The evidence

Five arm's-length MLS sales from September 2025 to February 2026, bracketing the January 1, 2026 lien date. They sold for $999,000 to $1,500,000 (median $1,050,000). The strongest was an identical floorplan two doors down that sold for $1,050,000 six weeks after the lien date.

The homeowner also reported condition factors: a 22-year-old roof, termite damage in one wall, and a 2008 solar array running at about a quarter of its capacity.

The filing

Filed on October 12 through the Santa Cruz County e-file portal: the online application, a Statement of Facts attached at the e-signature step, and the $50 fee. The deadline was November 30.

We also spotted that no Homeowner's Exemption was on file. Claiming it is worth roughly another $86 a year.

What it means

The savings figure is an estimate. The appeal is waiting for a decision, and the board could raise, lower, or keep the assessment. Every home is different. This is one pilot case, not a typical result. Savings were estimated at the property's effective tax rate of 1.23%.
